# Vindication

![Virtue](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-virtue-blue)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Vindication is the virtue concerned with the morally ordered response to wrongdoing. Its purpose is not the satisfaction of anger or the infliction of suffering for its own sake, but the restoration and protection of justice through proportionate correction.

Aquinas treats vindication as a virtue annexed to justice because justice concerns what is due to another. When someone has committed an injury or wrong, an appropriate response may be due for the protection of the good and the correction of wrongdoing.

Vindication must therefore be distinguished from vindictiveness. The morality of the act depends upon its intention, manner, and circumstances. Punishment sought primarily out of hatred or delight in another's suffering is vicious; correction directed toward a just good can belong to virtue.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.108.
