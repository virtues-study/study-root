# Affability

![Virtue](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-virtue-blue)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Affability is the social virtue by which a person relates appropriately to others in ordinary words and actions. It disposes a person to behave toward others in a fitting and agreeable manner.

Aquinas distinguishes this virtue from friendship based upon personal affection. Affability does not require that every person with whom we interact actually be our friend. Rather, it concerns the outward conduct appropriate to human social life.

It belongs to the family of justice because human beings owe one another a certain fittingness of social behavior. Yet this obligation is not normally a strict legal debt. Affability therefore falls short of justice in the strict sense and is annexed to justice as a secondary virtue.

Affability does not mean simply pleasing everyone. Its virtuous mean must avoid both excessive pleasing of others and unnecessarily contentious behavior.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.114.
