# Epikeia

![Virtue](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-virtue-blue)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Epikeia, commonly translated as equity, is the virtue by which a person follows the intention of justice and the common good when the literal application of a human law would produce an unjust or harmful result.

Human laws must be expressed through general rules. Aquinas observes, however, that human actions involve innumerable particular circumstances. A legislator therefore cannot formulate a rule that correctly covers every exceptional situation.

Epikeia does not reject law or permit a person simply to disregard laws with which he disagrees. Rather, it recognizes an exceptional case in which following the letter of the law would contradict the very justice and common good that the law was intended to serve.

Aquinas gives the example of property entrusted for safekeeping. Ordinarily justice requires that a deposit be returned to its owner. But if a person in a state of madness demands the return of a deposited sword, returning it could cause serious harm. In such a case the purpose of justice is better served by departing from the literal rule.

Epikeia therefore concerns the proper application of law according to its rational purpose rather than mere adherence to its wording.

Aquinas considers epikeia a subjective part of justice and associates it especially with legal or general justice. Indeed, when legal justice is understood merely as observance of the letter of the law, epikeia stands above it insofar as it directs legal observance according to the higher requirements of justice and the common good.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.120, aa.1-2.
