# Detraction

![Vice](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-vice-red)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Detraction is the unjust injury of another person's reputation through words spoken about that person to others. Aquinas treats it as a sin against justice because a person's good name is a good that can be unjustly taken away or diminished.

Detraction differs from direct insult or reviling. Reviling attacks a person's honor openly and directly, whereas detraction damages a person's reputation in the estimation of other people, typically in the person's absence.

Importantly, detraction does not require that what is said be false. A person may unjustly damage another's reputation even by revealing something true when there is no proportionate reason for making it known. If the damaging statement is false, an additional offense against truth is involved.

Aquinas regards reputation as important because human beings live in community and depend upon the esteem and trust of others for many aspects of social life. To deprive someone unjustly of a good reputation can therefore cause serious harm.

The moral evaluation of speech about another person consequently involves more than asking whether the statement is factually true. Justice also requires consideration of whether one has sufficient reason to disclose information that will damage another's good name.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.73.
