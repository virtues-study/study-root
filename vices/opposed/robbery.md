# Robbery

![Vice](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-vice-red)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Robbery is the unjust taking of another person's property by violence or force. Aquinas distinguishes robbery from theft according to the manner in which another's property is taken: theft involves taking secretly, whereas robbery involves taking openly through violence.

Like theft, robbery violates justice because it deprives another person of what is rightfully theirs. It therefore has a particular connection with commutative justice, which governs what is due between individuals and seeks equality in their dealings.

Aquinas considers robbery especially serious because the wrong involves not only the taking of another's property but also violence against the person from whom it is taken. Thus robbery adds an injury against the person to the injustice concerning external possessions.

Aquinas uses the Latin term *rapina*, often translated as robbery or rapine, for this form of unjust taking.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.66, aa.4, 8-9.
