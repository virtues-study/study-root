# Unjust Judgment

![Vice](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-vice-red)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Unjust judgment is a violation of justice occurring in the exercise of judgment, particularly judicial judgment. Aquinas treats the judge as responsible for rendering what is just between parties according to the authority and standards proper to judgment.

Because judgment determines what is due to another in a dispute, judging is fundamentally an act of justice. A corrupt or improperly rendered judgment therefore directly violates the virtue whose task is to give each person what is due.

Aquinas considers several ways in which judgment may become unjust. A person may judge without legitimate authority, may render judgment contrary to justice, or may base judgment upon insufficient grounds rather than the evidence appropriate to the judicial process.

His treatment also emphasizes that judicial judgment has its own proper order. A judge does not act merely according to private knowledge or personal preference but within a public office and according to the evidence and procedures appropriate to that office.

Unjust judgment therefore represents more than simply making an intellectual mistake. In its proper moral sense, it concerns the failure to exercise the responsibility of judgment according to justice.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.67.
