# Usury

![Vice](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-vice-red)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Usury, in Aquinas's treatment, is the injustice involved in charging a borrower for the use of money lent when that use cannot be separated from the money itself.

Aquinas distinguishes between things whose use can be separated from their ownership and things that are consumed through their use. A house, for example, can be owned by one person while another pays for its use. Money, as Aquinas analyzes it in the context of a loan, belongs to the second category: its relevant use consists in spending or exchanging it.

On this account, requiring repayment of the money lent and also charging separately for its use amounts to charging twice for what Aquinas regards as the same thing. He therefore considers such a charge contrary to justice.

Aquinas nevertheless recognizes that a lender can suffer a genuine loss through making a loan. His discussion allows compensation in certain circumstances for a loss incurred by the lender, while distinguishing such compensation from charging simply for the use of the money itself.

Usury belongs particularly to the domain of commutative justice because lending establishes obligations between individuals. The central moral concern is whether the exchange preserves what is justly due between lender and borrower.

Aquinas's account should not be identified without qualification with every modern practice involving interest. Modern financial arrangements, monetary systems, investment, risk, inflation, opportunity cost, and the distinction between productive investment and a simple loan require further analysis beyond the economic framework assumed in the medieval discussion. The node records Aquinas's account of usury rather than attempting by itself to classify every contemporary financial transaction.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.78.
