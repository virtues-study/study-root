# False Testimony

![Vice](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-vice-red)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

False testimony is the giving of testimony contrary to the truth, particularly when one's testimony bears upon the rights, reputation, or judgment of another person.

Aquinas treats testimony within the context of judicial justice because a witness participates in the process by which a judge determines what is due. The witness therefore has an obligation to speak truthfully concerning what is relevant to the matter being judged.

False testimony can violate justice because it may deprive another person of what is due through an unjust judgment produced or influenced by false evidence. At the same time, it violates truthfulness because the witness deliberately represents as true something that is false.

The gravity of false testimony is therefore not limited to the false statement itself. Its consequences may include injury to another person's rights, reputation, property, or even life when judicial decisions depend upon the testimony given.

Aquinas also considers the obligation of witnesses to testify when testimony is required according to justice. The responsibilities surrounding testimony therefore concern both truthfulness and the duty owed to others within the judicial process.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.70.
