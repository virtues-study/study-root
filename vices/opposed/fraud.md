# Fraud

![Vice](https://img.shields.io/badge/type-vice-red)
![Justice](https://img.shields.io/badge/family-justice-white)

## Definition

Fraud is a violation of justice in exchange in which deception is used to obtain an unjust advantage over another person. Aquinas treats fraudulent buying and selling as contrary to the equality that should govern voluntary exchanges.

Commutative justice requires a form of equality between what one person gives and what another receives. Fraud undermines this equality by manipulating the transaction through deception, causing one party to give or receive something under false or misleading conditions.

Aquinas considers several ways an exchange can become unjust. These include selling something for more than its just worth or buying it for less through unjust means, misrepresenting the quantity of goods, and concealing defects that materially affect what is being exchanged.

Aquinas's discussion of hidden defects is particularly nuanced. A seller may not deceive a buyer about a defect that makes the object dangerous, unusable for its intended purpose, or worth substantially less than represented. Justice in exchange therefore involves not only the transfer of property but also truthful dealing concerning what is being exchanged.

Fraud consequently has connections both to commutative justice and to truthfulness. Its primary injustice, however, lies in using deception to disturb the equality and what is due between the parties to an exchange.

The term "fraud" here refers specifically to Aquinas's treatment of deceptive practices in buying and selling and should not automatically be identified with every offense classified as fraud in modern civil or criminal law.

**Primary reference:** *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.77.
